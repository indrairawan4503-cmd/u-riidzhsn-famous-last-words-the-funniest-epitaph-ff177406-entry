# VERSIONS.md — variant B

## Concept
Modern mundane last words: a tech-era dying wish. Dave treats the most
trivially digital details of his life — the Wi-Fi password, his Netflix
login, his free trials — with full tombstone gravity. The joke turns on the
ironic reversal: the generous bequest (the Wi-Fi password, shared) is
immediately undercut by the petty one (the Netflix password dies with him),
and the final beat lands on the smallest possible earthly worry — a man on
his deathbed fretting about a free trial renewing. One concrete scene, one
surprising turn, carved like stone.

## Iterations

- **v1 — 105 chars:** `Here lies Tom. His last words: 'The Wi-Fi password is on the fridge — the Netflix password dies with me.'`
  Core idea found, but flat: one beat only, no escalation, and "Tom" is a placeholder.
- **v2 — 115 chars:** `Here lies Tom, 1988–2026. His last words: 'The Wi-Fi password is on the fridge. The Netflix password dies with me.'`
  Dates added for tombstone gravity; the em dash became a full stop for a stonier rhythm.
- **v3 — 111 chars:** `Here lies Dave, 1982–2026. His last words: 'Wi-Fi password's on the fridge. The Netflix password dies with me.'`
  Tighter, more spoken: "Dave" and the contracted "password's" sound like real last words, but still one beat short.
- **v4 — 128 chars:** `Here lies Dave, 1982–2026. Last words: 'Wi-Fi password's on the fridge. Netflix password dies with me. Cancel my subscriptions.'`
  Third beat added, but "Cancel my subscriptions" is vague and "Last words:" drops the warmer "His".
- **v5 — 130 chars:** `Here lies Dave, 1982–2026. His last words: 'Wi-Fi password's on the fridge. Netflix password dies with me. Cancel my free trials.'`
  "Cancel my free trials" is the sharper third beat — a dying man scared of a $0 charge. Dropped "The" before Netflix, which reads slightly rushed.
- **v6 — 134 chars:** `Here lies Dave, 1982–2026. His last words: 'Wi-Fi password's on the fridge. The Netflix password dies with me. My inbox stays unread.'`
  Third beat swapped to unread inbox; funny, but passive — a shrug, not a dying wish.
- **v7 — 123 chars:** `Here lies Dave, 1982–2026. His last words: 'Wi-Fi password's on the fridge. Netflix password dies with me. Tell IT I quit.'`
  Third beat swapped to quitting his job; punchy, but it changes the subject from digital legacy to work.
- **v8 — 134 chars (FINAL):** `Here lies Dave, 1982–2026. His last words: 'Wi-Fi password's on the fridge. The Netflix password dies with me. Cancel my free trials.'`
  Restores "The" before Netflix for a steadier cadence and keeps the best third beat from v5. Three escalating beats: generous bequest → petty hoarding → absurdly trivial final worry.
